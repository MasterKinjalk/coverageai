## Verdict

Inside the simulator the learned feedback is a slightly more accurate correction than the eq. 3 rule, and LightGBM and XGBoost give the same result (their shares on target differ by less than 0.003): with the noisy reader and mismatch low + med + high, mean |true - corrected| falls by 0.12 pp [0.00, 0.22] with one nozzle per field (5.23 to 5.11 pp, against a per-card-noise floor of 4.84 pp) and by 0.13 pp [-0.03, +0.26] with free nozzles (CI includes zero). Sections on target rise by 2.1 pp [1.3, 2.9] (one nozzle) and 2.7 pp [2.1, 3.3] (free), which matches the fair oracle (gap recovery 101 % [92, 113] and 105 % [96, 119], against 48 % and 74 % for the rule). The cost is a correction that is on average 0.6 pp more negative, so more spray: volume +0.24 GPA [0.14, 0.34] (about +1 %) with one nozzle and +0.17 GPA [-0.05, +0.39] free, and with one nozzle per field drift rises by 0.36 percentage points [0.16, 0.59] (about +16 % relative, +0.80 at high mismatch) because the held nozzle can only add coverage through pressure and finer droplets; with free nozzles drift rises by only 0.02 points. With no hidden mismatch the learned correction is less accurate than no correction at all (5.11 against 5.00 pp for open loop, one nozzle) but less wrong than the rule (5.30), and its learned prior of about -2.5 pp before the first card makes it spray more than needed there. The post-hoc controls (see the section above) split the gain: against the rule shifted by the validation mean difference, 0.9 pp [0.6, 1.3] (one nozzle) and 1.7 pp [1.1, 2.3] (free) of the 2.1 and 2.7 pp remain, so roughly half of the gain on target is caution and half is information; a plain mean of the pass's card errors with the learned starting value does as well as XGBoost under mismatch (difference -0.1 pp, CI includes zero) at more drift with one nozzle (+0.09 points) and more volume with free nozzles (+0.50 GPA), and worse with no mismatch. The 5-seed b0 check points the same way on target (+1.3 pp [0.7, 2.0]) at +0.25 GPA, with no measurable accuracy gain (-0.02 pp [-0.32, +0.38]). A post-hoc adoption check over other app options (section above) finds the trained feedback no worse than the rule on target anywhere, but it over-corrects in two places: combined with the slow loop (fast_slow), where the corrections stack (total -5.3 pp) and the correction becomes less accurate than the rule's, and at c_req 40, where volume rises by 7 % and drift by 54 %.

## What was not tested

- Real cards or real fields. The models learned the simulator's own hidden losses (canopy interception, nozzle wear, AR(1) gusts), which are of a few known kinds.
- Other lambda values. Targets other than 20, 30 and 40 %. The adoption-check options (fast_slow, speed and boom-height control, the ar1 forecaster) were tested one at a time with the noisy reader and one nozzle per field only: not combined with each other, not with free nozzles, the perfect reader or the image readers, and not with other forecasters.
- The image readers beyond the 5-seed b0 check (b1 not run). The training rows came from the perfect and noisy readers only.
- Kinds of mismatch outside the training distribution (for example a stronger canopy loss than the 'high' level, or a reader with a different bias). A trained model can fail there in ways the rule does not.
- The two controls (rule + constant, pass mean + learned prior) were run only after the headline had been seen, on the evaluation seeds, with the noisy reader only (not perfect, not b0), and their constants were not tuned; they are a diagnostic, not a validated alternative. A pass-mean rule chosen on validation seeds and evaluated on fresh seeds would be needed before preferring it to the trained model.
- The run time cost in the app: a learned correction is one tree-ensemble prediction per selection (pass times are in the json, `pass_s`).
