Everything here is simulation. The feedback models learned this simulator's hidden mismatch and card noise. The reader calibration subtracts the reader's mean error per coverage band, measured in the reader's own validation on synthetic cards with known truth; a real reader would need annotated real cards, and a real feedback model real verified cards.

Metrics use the true outcome. Each metric is averaged over the presets within a seed; brackets are 95 % bootstrap CIs over the 20 seeds, paired for differences; `*` marks a difference whose CI excludes zero. abs err = mean |true - model - correction|; signed err = mean (true - model - correction), positive = the loop expected less coverage than it got (over-applied correction); card - true = mean reading error of the reader in use. ## Verdict

On fresh seeds 100 to 119, run once after v2 was frozen (mismatch low + med + high, one nozzle per field, noisy reader), the new default (v2 XGBoost with the calibrated reader) puts more sections on target than the old default (rule with the raw reader) at every target. The gain is +6.0 pp [5.1, 6.9] at c_req 20, +1.5 pp [0.8, 2.3] at 30 and +6.8 pp [4.7, 8.9] at 40. Its correction is more accurate: |true - corrected| is -0.08, -0.23 and -0.29 pp, all CIs exclude zero.

The cost grows with the target. Against the old default, volume rises by +0.57, +0.10 and +1.11 GPA and drift by +0.21, +0.19 and +1.12 percentage points. Against the fair oracle, v2 is level at c_req 30 (volume -0.03, drift +0.05, both CIs include zero). It sprays more at 20 (+0.32 GPA, +0.18 drift points) and at 40 (+0.70 GPA, +0.65 drift points, about +13 % drift), where it also meets the target more often than the oracle (0.973 against 0.965).

Most of the gain is the model's own: against the rule with the same calibrated reader it adds +4.6, +2.0 and +9.4 pp on target. Calibration alone helps the rule only at c_req 20 (+1.4 pp). At 30 and 40 it lowers the rule's sections on target (-0.5 and -2.6 pp) and its spray, because the uncalibrated cards had been pushing the rule to over-apply. Retraining on calibrated cards (v2 against v1, both calibrated) changes little: on target -0.1, +0.6 and -0.7 pp, with less volume (-0.31 GPA) and drift (-0.26 points) at c_req 40.

Two problems remain. With no hidden mismatch, v2 still over-applies: the signed error is +0.6 to +1.0 pp, from its learned starting value of about -2.4 pp. At c_req 40 that costs +1.46 GPA and +1.22 drift points (about +52 %) against the oracle, though only +0.22 GPA and +0.29 points against the old default. And with the classic image reader b0 (5 seeds), the calibration table from synthetic cards does not remove the reader's bias in the loop; it reverses it (card - true +0.01 pp raw against +0.60 calibrated at c_req 30, -1.18 against +0.94 at 40). v2 still beats the old default there on target (+1.8 and +5.7 pp) and on accuracy (-0.3 pp), at +1.0 GPA and +0.8 drift points at c_req 40.

The perfect reader gives the same picture as the calibrated noisy reader, so the model does not rely on reader bias. With the slow loop (fast_slow), the trained model still stacks with the slow loop's offset. Against plain fast with v2, the signed error is +1.50 pp [1.02, 1.96], the error +0.16 pp [0.02, 0.33] larger and drift +0.38 points, which supports keeping the rule there (as the library already does, D31).

## What was not tested

- Real cards, real readers and real fields. The calibration table and the hidden mismatch are the simulator's own; the noisy reader follows the table exactly, so calibration works perfectly by construction for it. The b0 check is the only test against a reader that does not follow the table by construction.
- A calibration table that is wrong or out of date (reader bias that drifts with lighting, card batch or droplet size).
- The b1 (U-Net) reader; other lambda values; targets other than 20, 30 and 40 %; the options combined with each other or with free nozzles; other forecasters.
- Free nozzle at c_req 20 and 40; the v1 models at c_req 20/40 with the raw reader on fresh seeds.
