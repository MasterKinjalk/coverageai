2 Technical Progress and Results

2.1 Problem and system as built

Sprayers commit to settings before they see how weather and machine state change the deposit, and standard water-sensitive-paper (WSP) checks report problems only after the sprayer has passed. The proposal closes that loop: before each field segment a model predicts coverage and drift for every candidate setting, a selector picks the setting that meets the coverage requirement Creq at the lowest predicted drift and volume, the sprayer applies it, computer vision reads the coverage actually achieved from a WSP card, and the error corrects the next segments (fast loop) and the model between passes (slow loop). Without spray trials this semester, the whole loop runs in a physics-informed simulator with injected hidden mismatch (wind gusts, canopy interception, nozzle wear) that the model never sees.

The loop follows the proposal (@fig:arch). Modules exchange versioned records defined in contracts.py (schema 1.2: optional travel speed and boom height added to the 1.0 setting). loop.py runs a 40-segment pass under six policies (open, fast, fast_slow, fair oracle, card-aware oracle_card, best_fixed) and logs one row per segment. All extensions are opt-in and default off; with them off, results are bit-identical to the baseline. The code base is {{src_lines}} lines of Python with {{n_tests}} test functions; the full suite ({{n_suite}} tests after parametrization) passes ({{suite_machine}}). All proposal modules are built, and the M3 and part of the M4 work is done ahead of schedule (Section 5.1).

2.2 Nozzle catalog

data/catalog/nozzles.csv holds 316 chart rows for five families (TP65 50, XR 72, DGX40 24, ULD 80, 3D 90; 43 family-size combinations). catalog.py interpolates flow along Q ∝ √P, recomputes GPA at 20 in spacing, and generates 547 candidate settings on a 5 psi grid. The validator reports 0 violations on four checks (square-root law, sampled GPA cells, droplet class monotone in pressure, ranges). Compromises: TP65 droplet class uses the 80° column (D3), DGX40 contributes flow only (D4), ULD and 3D rows come from manufacturer sheets [12], spray angles are nominal (D5).

2.3 Simulator

The simulator composes first-order relationships and is not a validated drift model. Flow follows Q = Qref√(P/Pref), application rate GPA = 5940·GPM/(MPH·W) [4]. The volume spectrum is log-normal with VMD set by the ASABE S572.3 class [3]. Terminal velocity uses a Schiller–Naumann drag correlation [9]; evaporation follows the d2 law [10] with Stull's wet-bulb approximation [11]. Droplets leave the nozzle at v0 = 0.6√(2P/ρw) and relax toward Vt under linear drag (D10); size bins displaced over 0.5 m cross-track are drift. Coverage uses a Boolean (Poisson) model [5],

C = 1 − exp(−N·E[πs2/4]/A),   s = SF·d,   SF ~ U[1.6, 2.2] per card [6],   d ≥ 50 µm.

The per-card spread factor SF is the irreducible noise that recurs throughout this report: it is drawn when the card is sprayed, so no controller can know it in advance. Hidden mismatch (AR(1) gusts, canopy interception, nozzle wear) is set by one level (none, low, med, high) and never seen by the model. Tests cover the anchors in @tab:anchors, monotone trends (@fig:trends), the Boolean formula against Monte Carlo placement, and seeded determinism.

2.5 Selector and scenarios

The selector implements Eq. (2) of the proposal: it scores every catalog candidate, keeps those with Ĉ + b − m ≥ Creq, and minimizes λ·driftn + (1 − λ)·volumen (min-max normalized over the feasible set; λ = 0.6). The margin m is the lower half-width of the prediction interval, floored at 1.5 pp; if nothing is feasible it returns the highest-coverage setting and flags the segment. The scenario library generates 40-segment passes with AR(1) wind and gust bursts, rotating wind direction, diurnal temperature and humidity, and back-and-forth headings, in six presets (calm_morning, gusty_afternoon, hot_dry, crosswind_shift, marginal_limits, inversion_evening); the parameters are plausible regime choices, not measured weather statistics.

2.6 WSP renderer and public data

The renderer turns the droplets on a card into an RGB image and an exact mask (76 × 26 mm, 600 dpi). Each detectable droplet leaves an area-preserving elliptical stain of diameter SF·d; degradations (warp, illumination, blur, noise, JPEG) come in scanner and phone profiles (@fig:wsp). Over 100 undegraded cards, mask coverage matches the analytic Boolean value to a mean absolute 0.14 pp. The Simões et al. record [7] provides {{simoes_n}} synthetic cards with polygon labels (CC BY 4.0; {{simoes_droplets}} droplets; coverage {{simoes_cov_min}}% to {{simoes_cov_max}}%, mean {{simoes_cov_mean}}%), which we use as an out-of-generator test.

2.7 Post-spray verification

B0 is a classical pipeline: card detection with perspective correction, illumination flattening, an (R−B) stain channel thresholded with Otsu's method [2,17], opening, and a distance-transform watershed for stain counts and Dv0.1/0.5/0.9. B1 is a U-Net with a lightweight pretrained encoder [8] that segments stains on tiles; coverage is the stained share of the mask. Both flag saturated cards. Coverage, not stain counts, is the quantity the loop uses: count errors are large for both readers wherever stains touch.

4 Analysis and Reasoning

4.1 What the results show and do not show

  • The loop does what it was designed to do, inside the simulator. With injected mismatch the fast + slow loop recovers {{fs_rec}} of the gap between the open loop and the fair oracle at {{fs_vol}} volume (Section 2.8). This shows that the control logic corrects a model that is wrong in a known way. It does not show field efficacy: plant, training data, renderer and mismatch come from one simulator.
  • Most of the remaining shortfall is noise that no controller can remove. Three independent results point the same way: the within-pass residual variance does not shrink over five passes (Section 2.8); a perfect gust forecast does not raise success (Section 2.9.3); and the fair oracle, which knows every hidden condition except the per-card spread factor, still uses {{fair_over_card}} more volume than the card-aware oracle. The volume target against the card-aware oracle is therefore unreachable by construction, and we report the fair oracle as the reference.
  • The calibrated interval, not the point prediction, makes the selector reliable. The learned model with its conformal interval meets the requirement more often than the exact simulator physics used without an interval (Section 2.5), because the margin has to cover card noise.
  • More control freedom did not translate into less drift with the deployable models. With the fair oracle under mismatch, boom-height control cuts drift at no loss in success; with the nominal and learned models the selector spends the height freedom mainly on volume (Section 2.9.2), and our data do not isolate the cause further. Travel speed gives a small open-loop drift gain but has no closed-loop result with a CI (Section 2.9.1). The hybrid predictor repairs a wrong physics base offline and in the selector but is not better than pure ML in the loop (Section 2.9.4).
  • Card reading is the weakest link for a real deployment. Both readers meet the targets on our own cards, but on out-of-generator cards B1 drops to {{b1s_mae}} pp MAE and {{b1s_agree}}% class agreement (Section 2.7), and the real-card criterion is untested. The loop's numbers use a noise model calibrated to B0, so they assume a reader at least that good.

4.3 Key decisions and why

The decision log (Docs/decisions.md) holds {{n_decisions}} entries, each resolving an ambiguity that would have split the modules or recording a design choice with its evaluation verdict. D1–D5 fix conventions and catalog gaps; D6 deferred speed control; D7–D8 record the compressed schedule and the Python toolchain; D9 reports the real-card criterion as untested rather than estimating it. D10 adds nozzle exit velocity: with the first-order displacement x = U·H/Vt a medium droplet drifted 87% at 2 m/s, which is implausible; with exit velocity and linear-drag relaxation it drifts 8%, without retuning the 0.5 m threshold, so the fix changes the physics, not a free parameter. D11 and D12 add travel speed and boom height as optional settings, D13 the wind forecaster and D14 the hybrid predictor, each with its evaluation verdict. D15 makes the fair oracle the reference of the closed-loop criterion, because the first oracle saw per-card noise that no controller can know; D16 replaces the first slow loop (a LightGBM refit that fitted card noise) with a shrunk residual offset on the frozen model. Both D15 and D16 were fixed on dev seeds before the evaluation grid ran, so the criterion was not tuned on the seeds that test it. D17 exposes the extensions in the demo app as opt-in controls.

4.4 Limitations

  • Simulator-internal and partly circular. Plant, training data, renderer and hidden mismatch come from one hand-built simulator, fit to published trends, not a validated drift model. The experiments test whether the control logic behaves as designed when the model is wrong in a known, low-dimensional way; they do not measure field efficacy. The mismatch is injected by us and is of the kind the bias and offset are built to absorb (a coverage offset that persists over a pass, with a mean that persists across passes). Inversions, boom roll, nozzle clogging and card-placement error are not tested. Scenarios are a synthetic generator, not weather records. The hybrid's exact physics base is the plant itself, so its exact-base rows are an upper bound.
  • The oracle defines the result. Against the fair oracle the closed-loop criterion is met; against the card-aware oracle it is missed on volume. With a stricter fair oracle (q = 0.05) recovery shrinks but the criterion still holds. The fair-oracle definition and its q = 0.1 risk level were fixed before the grid ran.
  • No real annotated cards. The two annotated real cards are in a restricted Zenodo record (D9). The real-card criterion (≤ 5 pp) is untested, and sim-to-real transfer of B0 and B1 is unmeasured; the proposal cites a drop from 57.06% to 40.81% for YOLOv8 from synthetic to real cards [7].
  • Out-of-generator class agreement is missed. On the Simões synthetic cards B1 reaches {{b1s_mae}} pp MAE but {{b1s_agree}}% class agreement (B0: {{b0s_mae}} pp, {{b0s_agree}}%), against a 90% target. Both underread at 15–50% coverage, where few cards exist, and the Simões pixel scale is inferred.
  • The default loop verifier is a noise model. The closed-loop grid uses true coverage plus Gaussian error calibrated to B0, which omits heavy-tailed failures and domain shift; the rendered-card runs have only 5 seeds, and the grid covers four of the six presets.
  • Same class, same physics. Spray angle and pattern overlap are not modeled (beyond the boom-height overlap constraint), so families with the same droplet class and flow are indistinguishable to the selector.

5.3 Risks

The proposal's risks stand. Sim-to-real transfer is unmeasured until real cards arrive; the out-of-generator drop of B1 (Section 2.7) shows the risk is real. Circular evaluation is limited, not removed, by the hidden mismatch, the fair oracle, the misspecified physics bases and the perfect-measurement ablation. Integration slip was realized as the M1 and M2 slip and has since been recovered. New since the proposal: AI-written code that teammates must understand (owner review; each member presents their own module at M5), and data access for the real cards, which depends on the dataset authors: access was requested through Zenodo and by email (reports/email_draft_simoes.md) and has not been granted yet, so the M3 plan includes a fallback (Section 6).
