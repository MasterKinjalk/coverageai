2 Technical Progress and Early Results

2.1 System architecture as built

The loop follows the proposal (@fig:arch). Modules exchange frozen, versioned records defined in contracts.py (schema 1.0), so each was built and tested against mocks first. loop.py runs a 40-segment pass under five policies (open, fast, fast_slow, oracle, best_fixed) and logs one row per segment. The code base is {{src_lines}} lines of Python with {{n_tests}} test functions.

2.2 Nozzle catalog

data/catalog/nozzles.csv holds 316 chart rows for the five families (TP65 50, XR 72, DGX40 24, ULD 80, 3D 90; 43 family-size combinations). catalog.py interpolates flow along Q ∝ √P, recomputes GPA at 20 in spacing, and generates 547 candidate settings on a 5 psi grid within each family's operating range. The validator reports 0 violations on four checks: flow within ±5% of the square-root law between adjacent rows (3 further deviations excused as chart rounding), GPA against 8 sampled chart cells, droplet class monotone in pressure, and ranges against spec.yaml. Compromises: TP65 droplet class uses the 80° column as proxy (D3), DGX40 contributes flow only (D4), ULD and 3D rows come from the manufacturers' sheets [12], and spray angles are nominal (D5).

2.3 Simulator physics

The simulator composes first-order relationships and is not a validated drift model. Flow follows Q = Qref√(P/Pref) and application rate follows GPA = 5940·GPM/(MPH·W) [4]. The volume spectrum is log-normal with VMD set by the ASABE S572.3 class [3] (centers F 145, M 215, C 310 µm; relative span 1.2). Terminal velocity Vt uses a Schiller–Naumann drag correlation [9]. Evaporation follows the d2 law with λ = 84.76·ΔTwb µm2/s [10] and Stull's wet-bulb approximation [11]; fully evaporated volume is lost. Droplets leave the nozzle at v0 = 0.6√(2P/ρw) (14.1 m/s at 40 psi) and relax toward Vt and the wind under linear drag (τ = Vt/g); size bins displaced over 0.5 m cross-track are drift. Coverage uses a Boolean (Poisson) model [5],

C = 1 − exp(−N·E[πs2/4]/A),   s = SF·d,   SF ~ U[1.6, 2.2] per segment [6],   d ≥ 50 µm.

Hidden mismatch (AR(1) gusts, canopy interception η ∈ [0.6, 1.0], nozzle wear) is set by one level (none, low, med, high) and never seen by the model. Tests cover the anchors in @tab:anchors, monotone trends (@fig:trends), the Boolean formula against Monte Carlo placement, and seeded determinism.

2.4 Dataset and predictive model

Dataset v1 has {{dataset_rows}} simulator rows: Latin-hypercube conditions over the full operating ranges, settings drawn uniformly from the 547 candidates, no hidden mismatch. Coverage has median 17.3% (10th to 90th percentile 5.4% to 46.3%); 43.5% of rows are critical, 30.9% marginal, 25.6% acceptable. Features are raw conditions, wind as cross-track and along-track components, catalog-derived flow, VMD and angle, and physics features (wet-bulb depression, H/Vt, |Ucross|H/Vt, GPA/VMD). We compare ridge, random forest and LightGBM [15]; the interval comes from quantile LightGBM with conformal calibration [16]. 2.5 Selector and scenarios

The selector implements Eq. (2) of the proposal: it scores every catalog candidate with the learned model (SprayModel, Section 2.4), keeps those with Ĉ + b − m ≥ Creq, and picks the one minimizing λ·driftn + (1 − λ)·volumen (min-max normalized over the feasible set; λ = 0.6). The margin m is the lower half-width of the prediction interval, floored at 1.5 pp. If nothing is feasible it returns the highest-coverage setting and flags the segment. best_fixed is the single setting with the highest success rate over the scenario set, ties broken by lowest drift.

{{selector_block}}

The scenario library generates 40-segment passes with AR(1) wind and gust bursts, rotating wind direction, diurnal temperature and humidity trends, and back-and-forth headings (the crosswind changes sign every 10 segments). Six presets each have an agronomic rationale: calm_morning (1.2 m/s base wind, benign reference), gusty_afternoon (4.0 m/s, gust probability 0.12), hot_dry (33 to 38 °C, RH 28% to 16%; hot, dry air speeds evaporation and raises drift potential [13]), crosswind_shift (wind direction rotates 140°), marginal_limits (6.0 m/s mean, near the label limit [14], exercises the infeasible path) and inversion_evening (0.6 m/s, cooling; a limitation test, since inversions are not modeled). The parameters are plausible regime choices, not measured weather statistics. The rationale text was drafted from Emery's specification and awaits his review TEAM TO CONFIRM.

2.6 WSP renderer and public data

The renderer turns the droplets on a card into an RGB image and an exact mask (76 × 26 mm, 600 dpi). Each detectable droplet leaves an area-preserving elliptical stain of diameter SF·d; a vectorized rasterizer replaces cv2.ellipse, which misses 10 to 15% of small stains' area. Degradations (warp applied to the mask too, illumination, blur, noise, JPEG) come in scanner and phone profiles (@fig:wsp). Over 100 undegraded cards at 1% to 80% coverage, mask coverage matches the analytic Boolean value to a mean absolute 0.14 pp (maximum 0.70 pp; test limit 2 pp), and worst-case render time is under 1 s per card.

The Simões et al. record 10.5281/zenodo.13995950 [7] provides {{simoes_n}} synthetic cards with polygon labels (CC BY 4.0; {{simoes_droplets}} droplets; coverage {{simoes_cov_min}}% to {{simoes_cov_max}}%, mean {{simoes_cov_mean}}%). vision/real_data.py rasterizes the polygons and tiles cards; pixel size is inferred from card width because no dpi is published. The two annotated real cards (2,754 droplets) are in a later version whose files are restricted (D9). Access must be requested through Zenodo TEAM TO CONFIRM once sent; an email to the authors is drafted. Until access is granted the real-card criterion is untested.

2.7 Post-spray verification

Baseline B0 is a classical pipeline: card detection with perspective correction, illumination flattening, an (R−B) stain channel thresholded with Otsu's method [2,17], 3×3 opening, and a distance-transform watershed for stain counts and Dv0.1/0.5/0.9. A card is flagged saturated above 60% coverage, or above 30% when one connected component holds over 70% of the stained area.

2.9 Demonstration

The Streamlit app app/main.py runs a 40-segment pass segment by segment and shows every decision: the conditions, the chosen setting and why (feasible candidates among the 547, top-5 ranking), the rendered card with the verifier's mask, and the predicted, corrected, measured and true coverage. It has three tabs. Live pass steps through one pass under a chosen preset, mismatch level, policy, model and verifier. Policy comparison runs chosen policies (default open, fast and oracle) on the same seed and hidden mismatch. Nozzle explorer shows catalog-derived quantities and the nominal simulator's response for a chosen nozzle.

4 Analysis and Reasoning

4.1 What the results show and do not show

  • The model works, but the 3 pp target sits close to a floor set by the simulator. Coverage depends on a per-row random spread factor, so no model can beat an MAE of {{noise_floor}} pp against sampled outcomes. LightGBM's {{lgb_rand_mae}} pp is {{gap_to_floor}} pp above that floor; against the noise-free expectation its MAE is {{lgb_exp_mae}} pp (ridge: {{ridge_exp_mae}} pp). Ridge's miss ({{ridge_rand_mae}} pp) reflects real nonlinearity, but a 3 pp target leaves almost no slack, so the final report will also give error against expected coverage.
  • Withholding regimes barely changes coverage error but does change drift and shift. Coverage MAE stays between {{grp_min}} and {{grp_max}} pp because coverage is governed by volume and droplet size, which training data span. Shift MAE grows from {{shift_rand}} mm to {{shift_u5}} mm (wind above 5 m/s withheld) and {{shift_3d}} mm (3D withheld); drift MAE from {{drift_rand}} to {{drift_u5}} (wind above 5 m/s), so drift estimates are least reliable at high wind, where they matter most.
  • Sensitivity sweeps pass ({{sens_pass}} of {{sens_total}}); they check direction, not magnitude.
  • B0 is accurate mid-range and degrades at high coverage. {{b0_analysis}}
  • The interval-based margin is what makes the selector reliable. With the simulator as the model and only the 1.5 pp margin floor, the selector meets the requirement in {{sel_oracle_success}}% of scenarios; with the learned model and its conformal interval it does in {{sel_spray_success}}%. The per-card spread factor is noise no model can predict, so the margin must come from a calibrated interval.
  • The closed-loop volume criterion compares against an oracle with information no deployable controller has. The oracle sees each card's spread factor and needs no margin, which alone costs +12.2% volume with no mismatch; only about 3 of the 15.2 points are due to mismatch correction. A fairer oracle, one that knows the mismatch but not the per-card noise, is planned for M4.
  • Evaluation is circular. Simulator, renderer, model and verifier share assumptions (spread-factor range, Boolean overlap, 50 µm cutoff), so good synthetic scores show consistency, not farm performance. The hidden mismatch, oracle policy and perfect-measurement ablation separate the loop's benefit from this circularity.

4.2 Key decisions and why

The decision log (Docs/decisions.md) holds ten entries, each resolving an ambiguity that would have split the modules. D1 and D2 fix conventions (wind direction is where the air moves toward; four distinctly named outputs, because "D" meant drift in the proposal and deposition in the specification). D3 to D5 record catalog gaps. D6 limits the selector to nozzle, size and pressure, deferring speed as a control variable. D7 and D8 record the compressed schedule and the use of system Python 3.14. D9 reports the real-card criterion as untested because the annotated cards are restricted, rather than estimating it.

D10, exit velocity. With the first-order displacement x = U·H/Vt and a 0.5 m threshold, a medium droplet (215 µm, Vt = 0.78 m/s) needs 0.64 s to fall 0.5 m and moves 1.3 m in a 2 m/s crosswind, so the model predicted 87% drift of medium-droplet volume, which is implausible at this height. Real droplets leave the nozzle at about 14 m/s and cross most of the height in tens of milliseconds before settling. Adding exit velocity with linear-drag relaxation gives 30% drift for fine, 8% for medium and 1% for coarse sprays at the same conditions. We kept the 0.5 m threshold instead of retuning it, so the fix changes the physics, not a free parameter.

4.3 Limitations

  • Same class, same physics. Spray angle and pattern overlap are not modeled, so families with the same droplet class and flow are indistinguishable and the selector's choice among them is arbitrary (planned fix in Section 6). The spread factor also sets the {{noise_floor}} pp coverage floor, and real SF depends on paper, droplet size and humidity.
  • No real annotated cards yet, so sim-to-real transfer of B0 and B1 is unmeasured; the proposal cites a drop from 57.06% to 40.81% for YOLOv8 from synthetic to real cards [7].
  • WSP physics limits. Droplets below about 50 µm leave no stain and accuracy falls at high coverage, as B0's errors above 50% show.
  • Inversions and canopy structure are not modeled; inversion_evening tests a limitation, and the drift constants are fitted to published trends, not measured.
