1 Summary

All planned modules are built, tested and merged: simulator, nozzle catalog (316 chart rows, 547 candidate settings), predictive model, selector, scenario library, water-sensitive-paper (WSP) renderer, classical (B0) and learned (B1) verifiers, feedback module, loop harness, integration, experiment grid and a Streamlit demo. Five of the seven criteria in Table 1 of the proposal are met inside our simulator: the simulator, the predictive model (coverage MAE 2.84 pp random and 2.80 to 2.98 pp on withheld regimes, ridge 3.32 pp), the selector (97.0% requirement-met, with lower drift and about half the volume of the matched fixed setting), synthetic-card verification (B0 MAE 1.44 pp; B1 0.42 pp from a 6-epoch CPU checkpoint) and end-to-end operation. The closed-loop criterion is missed: the fast loop recovers 58% [38, 69] of the open-loop-to-oracle gap but uses +15.2% [+13.7, +16.9] more volume than the oracle (limit 10%), mostly because the oracle sees the per-card spread factor, and the slow loop as implemented did not help (-6% [-44, 17] recovery). Still open are the full GPU training of B1 and the real-card criterion, which is untested because the annotated cards are restricted. All results come from our own simulator and renderer, not the field. Milestone M1 was 16 days late and M2 2 days late, but all modules were built and merged on Oct 18; the remaining M3 work is B1 GPU training, real-card evaluation and a slow-loop redesign.

2 Technical Progress and Early Results

2.1 System architecture as built

The loop follows the proposal (Figure 1). Modules exchange frozen, versioned records defined in contracts.py (schema 1.0), so each was built and tested against mocks first. loop.py runs a 40-segment pass under five policies (open, fast, fast_slow, oracle, best_fixed) and logs one row per segment. The code base is 6,125 lines of Python with 180 test functions.

2.2 Nozzle catalog

data/catalog/nozzles.csv holds 316 chart rows for the five families (TP65 50, XR 72, DGX40 24, ULD 80, 3D 90; 43 family-size combinations). catalog.py interpolates flow along Q ∝ √P, recomputes GPA at 20 in spacing, and generates 547 candidate settings on a 5 psi grid within each family's operating range. The validator reports 0 violations on four checks: flow within ±5% of the square-root law between adjacent rows (3 further deviations excused as chart rounding), GPA against 8 sampled chart cells, droplet class monotone in pressure, and ranges against spec.yaml. Compromises: TP65 droplet class uses the 80° column as proxy (D3), DGX40 contributes flow only (D4), ULD and 3D rows come from the manufacturers' sheets [12], and spray angles are nominal (D5).

2.3 Simulator physics

The simulator composes first-order relationships and is not a validated drift model. Flow follows Q = Qref√(P/Pref) and application rate follows GPA = 5940·GPM/(MPH·W) [4]. The volume spectrum is log-normal with VMD set by the ASABE S572.3 class [3] (centers F 145, M 215, C 310 µm; relative span 1.2). Terminal velocity Vt uses a Schiller–Naumann drag correlation [9]. Evaporation follows the d2 law with λ = 84.76·ΔTwb µm2/s [10] and Stull's wet-bulb approximation [11]; fully evaporated volume is lost. Droplets leave the nozzle at v0 = 0.6√(2P/ρw) (14.1 m/s at 40 psi) and relax toward Vt and the wind under linear drag (τ = Vt/g); size bins displaced over 0.5 m cross-track are drift. Coverage uses a Boolean (Poisson) model [5],

C = 1 − exp(−N·E[πs2/4]/A),   s = SF·d,   SF ~ U[1.6, 2.2] per segment [6],   d ≥ 50 µm.

Hidden mismatch (AR(1) gusts, canopy interception η ∈ [0.6, 1.0], nozzle wear) is set by one level (none, low, med, high) and never seen by the model. Tests cover the anchors in Table 1, monotone trends (Figure 2), the Boolean formula against Monte Carlo placement, and seeded determinism.

2.4 Dataset and predictive model

Dataset v1 has 50,000 simulator rows: Latin-hypercube conditions over the full operating ranges, settings drawn uniformly from the 547 candidates, no hidden mismatch. Coverage has median 17.3% (10th to 90th percentile 5.4% to 46.3%); 43.5% of rows are critical, 30.9% marginal, 25.6% acceptable. Features are raw conditions, wind as cross-track and along-track components, catalog-derived flow, VMD and angle, and physics features (wet-bulb depression, H/Vt, |Ucross|H/Vt, GPA/VMD). We compare ridge, random forest and LightGBM [15]; the interval comes from quantile LightGBM with conformal calibration [16]. Splits are a random 80/20 hold-out and three grouped splits withholding wind above 5 m/s, the whole 3D family, and the hot-dry regime (T > 35 °C, RH < 30%). LightGBM reaches a coverage MAE of 2.84 pp on the random hold-out (target 3 pp) against 3.32 pp for ridge and 3.23 pp for random forest, and is the best model on every split. Its 80% prediction interval covers 80.0% of held-out rows with mean width 9.0 pp. Scoring all 547 candidates takes 9.3 ms (median) and a 50,000-row refit takes 2.3 s, so the selector can run for every segment.

2.5 Selector and scenarios

The selector implements Eq. (2) of the proposal: it scores every catalog candidate with the learned model (SprayModel, Section 2.4), keeps those with Ĉ + b − m ≥ Creq, and picks the one minimizing λ·driftn + (1 − λ)·volumen (min-max normalized over the feasible set; λ = 0.6). The margin m is the lower half-width of the prediction interval, floored at 1.5 pp. If nothing is feasible it returns the highest-coverage setting and flags the segment. best_fixed is the single setting with the highest success rate over the scenario set, ties broken by lowest drift.

With the learned model the selector meets the requirement in 97.0% of the 200 scenarios (target ≥ 90%). The lowest-drift fixed setting that matches this success (XR 15 @ 45 psi, 98%) has mean drift 0.0167 against 0.0131 (21% lower) and applies 47.5 GPA against 24.8, so the selector uses about half the volume. The overall best fixed setting (TP65 15 @ 55 psi) reaches 100% but at drift 0.0171 and 52.5 GPA. One selection takes 20 ms including 17 ms of model time, so it can run for every segment. Raising λ from 0 to 1 moves the selector from 0.105 drift at 17.9 GPA to 0.0072 drift at 33.6 GPA, with success at or above 93.5% throughout (Figure 4).

Why the interval-based margin is needed. In config oracle the simulator both selects and scores (an expected-value model with no prediction interval), yet success is only 38.5%. Without an interval the selector keeps only the fixed 1.5 pp margin floor, and the per-segment random spread factor of the card pushes about half of the just-feasible choices below Creq. Its low drift (0.0106) and volume (17.3 GPA) are therefore bought with failures, not a better selector. The learned model's conformal interval supplies the margin that this noise requires, which is why the margin is taken from the interval and not set to a constant.

The scenario library generates 40-segment passes with AR(1) wind and gust bursts, rotating wind direction, diurnal temperature and humidity trends, and back-and-forth headings (the crosswind changes sign every 10 segments). Six presets each have an agronomic rationale: calm_morning (1.2 m/s base wind, benign reference), gusty_afternoon (4.0 m/s, gust probability 0.12), hot_dry (33 to 38 °C, RH 28% to 16%; hot, dry air speeds evaporation and raises drift potential [13]), crosswind_shift (wind direction rotates 140°), marginal_limits (6.0 m/s mean, near the label limit [14], exercises the infeasible path) and inversion_evening (0.6 m/s, cooling; a limitation test, since inversions are not modeled). The parameters are plausible regime choices, not measured weather statistics. The rationale text was drafted from Emery's specification and awaits his review TEAM TO CONFIRM.

2.6 WSP renderer and public data

The renderer turns the droplets on a card into an RGB image and an exact mask (76 × 26 mm, 600 dpi). Each detectable droplet leaves an area-preserving elliptical stain of diameter SF·d; a vectorized rasterizer replaces cv2.ellipse, which misses 10 to 15% of small stains' area. Degradations (warp applied to the mask too, illumination, blur, noise, JPEG) come in scanner and phone profiles (Figure 5). Over 100 undegraded cards at 1% to 80% coverage, mask coverage matches the analytic Boolean value to a mean absolute 0.14 pp (maximum 0.70 pp; test limit 2 pp), and worst-case render time is under 1 s per card.

The Simões et al. record 10.5281/zenodo.13995950 [7] provides 300 synthetic cards with polygon labels (CC BY 4.0; 635,839 droplets; coverage 1.5% to 41.0%, mean 13.0%). vision/real_data.py rasterizes the polygons and tiles cards; pixel size is inferred from card width because no dpi is published. The two annotated real cards (2,754 droplets) are in a later version whose files are restricted (D9). Access must be requested through Zenodo TEAM TO CONFIRM once sent; an email to the authors is drafted. Until access is granted the real-card criterion is untested.

2.7 Post-spray verification

Baseline B0 is a classical pipeline: card detection with perspective correction, illumination flattening, an (R−B) stain channel thresholded with Otsu's method [2,17], 3×3 opening, and a distance-transform watershed for stain counts and Dv0.1/0.5/0.9. A card is flagged saturated above 60% coverage, or above 30% when one connected component holds over 70% of the stained area.

On the 180 held-out synthetic test cards from the team's renderer (Table 4), B0 reaches a coverage MAE of 1.44 pp (target ≤ 2 pp), three-class agreement of 96.1% (target ≥ 90%) and a runtime of 0.29 s mean and 0.93 s maximum per card on a laptop CPU (target ≤ 2 s). Error is not uniform: in the 50-80+% band the MAE is 3.28 pp with a bias of -3.28 pp (67% of those cards are flagged saturated) and the worst single card is off by 12.8 pp. B0 underestimates coverage above 50% because stains merge. Stain counts and Dv values are unreliable (median relative count error 0.64 overall, 0.25 in the lowest band), which is why coverage, not counts, is the quantity the loop uses.

2.8 Closed-loop experiments

The grid (experiments/closed_loop.py) runs five policies over four presets (calm_morning, crosswind_shift, gusty_afternoon, hot_dry), four hidden-mismatch levels (none, low, med, high) and 20 seeds, 40 segments per pass at Creq = 30%, all inside the simulator. The headline model is the learned SprayModel; a run with the mismatch-free simulator physics as the model (oracle_nominal) gives the same picture (fast loop 60% [43, 71] recovery, +13.8% volume). The verifier is noisy: perfect coverage plus Gaussian error with per-band bias and standard deviation from the B0 results. Gap recovery is (closed − open)/(oracle − open) in the share of segments meeting Creq; volume overhead is mean applied GPA relative to the oracle. Headline numbers pool low, med and high mismatch; brackets are 95% paired-bootstrap CIs over seeds.

The closed-loop criterion is missed. The fast loop recovers 58% [38, 69] of the open-loop-to-oracle gap, which meets 50% on the point estimate but not with confidence, and uses +15.2% [+13.7, +16.9] more volume than the oracle against the 10% limit. The fast loop works as designed. The requirement-met share under mismatch stays at 0.94 (low 0.947, med 0.943, high 0.932; Table 6, Figure 6), while open loop falls from 0.954 (low) to 0.864 (med) and 0.762 (high) and the best fixed setting to 0.61 at high. Why the volume criterion is missed. With no mismatch at all the fast loop already uses +12.2% [11.2, 13.1] more volume than the oracle. The oracle sees the per-segment random spread factor of the card (irreducible noise, standard deviation about 5 pp of coverage) and needs no safety margin, whereas every model-based policy must keep a margin from its prediction interval. Only about 3 pp of the 15.2% comes from correcting the hidden mismatch. The same noise bounds how much of the residual a bias correction can remove: raw residual variance stays at about 27 to 29 pp2 inside a pass. The 10% limit therefore compares against a reference no deployable controller can match (Section 4.1).

The slow loop, as implemented, did not help. It refits SprayModel on the measured cards of earlier passes with record weight 30. It removes the average model error (mean raw residual at high mismatch −6.0 pp in pass 1, −1.8 pp in pass 3) and cuts volume, but requirement-met drops to 0.85 against 0.94 for the fast loop and the raw residual variance rises from about 27 to about 33 pp2. A 6-seed diagnostic with no mismatch (experiments/slow_loop_diagnostic.py) shows the 80% prediction interval narrowing after the refit (mean half-width 8.0 to 5.2 pp), true coverage falling below its lower bound in 21 to 27% of segments instead of 7.5%, and the MAE rising from 4.7 to 5.0–5.1 pp. A plausible cause, not a tested mechanism, is that a few dozen noisy, heavily weighted records are fitted too closely and the conformal calibration is not redone on them. Record weights 1, 5 and 30 give 36%, 10% and −20% recovery (Table 7); 30, the grid's value, is the worst. The slow loop needs a better update rule (Section 6).

Ablations (fast loop, gusty_afternoon, med mismatch, 20 seeds unless noted). A smaller α is cheaper (α = 0.1, 0.3, 0.6 give 45%, 59%, 54% recovery at +13.0%, +17.9%, +20.5% volume) at a small loss of reliability. A global bias recovers more (68%) than per-regime biases (59%) at +19.8% against +17.9% volume, plausibly because each of four regimes sees few cards before its warm-up gate opens; warm-up of 0, 2 or 4 cards (57%, 59%, 50%) differs by less than the CI. Perfect, noisy and rendered-B0 verifiers give 51%, 59% and 44% (B0: 5 seeds, CI [−200, 90]), inside the seed-to-seed spread, so measurement error is not what limits the loop. Retreat flags were never raised (0 in 1,600 learned-model passes).

What these runs do and do not show. Plant, model training data, renderer and hidden mismatch all come from one hand-built simulator, so the runs test whether the control logic behaves as designed when the model is wrong in a known, low-dimensional way; they do not measure field efficacy. We injected the mismatch (gust multiplier, canopy interception, nozzle wear), and it is of the kind the bias term is built to absorb; inversions, boom roll, clogging and card-placement error are not tested, and the default verifier omits heavy-tailed failures and real-card domain shift.

2.9 Demonstration

The Streamlit app app/main.py runs a 40-segment pass segment by segment and shows every decision: the conditions, the chosen setting and why (feasible candidates among the 547, top-5 ranking), the rendered card with the verifier's mask, and the predicted, corrected, measured and true coverage. It has three tabs. Live pass steps through one pass under a chosen preset, mismatch level, policy, model and verifier. Policy comparison runs chosen policies (default open, fast and oracle) on the same seed and hidden mismatch. Nozzle explorer shows catalog-derived quantities and the nominal simulator's response for a chosen nozzle.

4 Analysis and Reasoning

4.1 What the results show and do not show

  • The model works, but the 3 pp target sits close to a floor set by the simulator. Coverage depends on a per-row random spread factor, so no model can beat an MAE of 2.76 pp against sampled outcomes. LightGBM's 2.84 pp is 0.09 pp above that floor; against the noise-free expectation its MAE is 0.64 pp (ridge: 1.82 pp). Ridge's miss (3.32 pp) reflects real nonlinearity, but a 3 pp target leaves almost no slack, so the final report will also give error against expected coverage.
  • Withholding regimes barely changes coverage error but does change drift and shift. Coverage MAE stays between 2.80 and 2.98 pp because coverage is governed by volume and droplet size, which training data span. Shift MAE grows from 3.4 mm to 8.3 mm (wind above 5 m/s withheld) and 12.2 mm (3D withheld); drift MAE from 0.0030 to 0.0051 (wind above 5 m/s), so drift estimates are least reliable at high wind, where they matter most.
  • Sensitivity sweeps pass (5 of 5); they check direction, not magnitude.
  • B0 is accurate mid-range and degrades at high coverage. B0 meets all three synthetic targets, but its error is 3.28 pp in the 50-80+% band against 0.70 pp in the lowest band (WSP saturation, as the proposal anticipated). Class thresholds sit at 15% and 30%, so this rarely changes the class; it would matter for a requirement above 50%.
  • The interval-based margin is what makes the selector reliable. With the simulator as the model and only the 1.5 pp margin floor, the selector meets the requirement in 38.5% of scenarios; with the learned model and its conformal interval it does in 97.0%. The per-card spread factor is noise no model can predict, so the margin must come from a calibrated interval.
  • The closed-loop volume criterion compares against an oracle with information no deployable controller has. The oracle sees each card's spread factor and needs no margin, which alone costs +12.2% volume with no mismatch; only about 3 of the 15.2 points are due to mismatch correction. A fairer oracle, one that knows the mismatch but not the per-card noise, is planned for M4.
  • Evaluation is circular. Simulator, renderer, model and verifier share assumptions (spread-factor range, Boolean overlap, 50 µm cutoff), so good synthetic scores show consistency, not farm performance. The hidden mismatch, oracle policy and perfect-measurement ablation separate the loop's benefit from this circularity.

4.2 Key decisions and why

The decision log (Docs/decisions.md) holds ten entries, each resolving an ambiguity that would have split the modules. D1 and D2 fix conventions (wind direction is where the air moves toward; four distinctly named outputs, because "D" meant drift in the proposal and deposition in the specification). D3 to D5 record catalog gaps. D6 limits the selector to nozzle, size and pressure, deferring speed as a control variable. D7 and D8 record the compressed schedule and the use of system Python 3.14. D9 reports the real-card criterion as untested because the annotated cards are restricted, rather than estimating it.

D10, exit velocity. With the first-order displacement x = U·H/Vt and a 0.5 m threshold, a medium droplet (215 µm, Vt = 0.78 m/s) needs 0.64 s to fall 0.5 m and moves 1.3 m in a 2 m/s crosswind, so the model predicted 87% drift of medium-droplet volume, which is implausible at this height. Real droplets leave the nozzle at about 14 m/s and cross most of the height in tens of milliseconds before settling. Adding exit velocity with linear-drag relaxation gives 30% drift for fine, 8% for medium and 1% for coarse sprays at the same conditions. We kept the 0.5 m threshold instead of retuning it, so the fix changes the physics, not a free parameter.

4.3 Limitations

  • Same class, same physics. Spray angle and pattern overlap are not modeled, so families with the same droplet class and flow are indistinguishable and the selector's choice among them is arbitrary (planned fix in Section 6). The spread factor also sets the 2.76 pp coverage floor, and real SF depends on paper, droplet size and humidity.
  • No real annotated cards yet, so sim-to-real transfer of B0 and B1 is unmeasured; the proposal cites a drop from 57.06% to 40.81% for YOLOv8 from synthetic to real cards [7].
  • WSP physics limits. Droplets below about 50 µm leave no stain and accuracy falls at high coverage, as B0's errors above 50% show.
  • Inversions and canopy structure are not modeled; inversion_evening tests a limitation, and the drift constants are fitted to published trends, not measured.
